Road Quality Map

M.Tech dissertation · open source · 2025 to 2026
↗

A dashcam records the road on an ordinary drive. This turns that footage into a map of how good or bad the surface actually is, stretch by stretch, and can then route you along the smoothest way rather than the shortest. It works out where the camera was from the timestamp burned into the video, and learns to recognize a rough surface or a pothole from the picture itself. It now covers 808 road segments. Fixing the data and the class design, rather than the model, took validation accuracy from 53% to 79.6%.

Why it matters

Road condition surveys are expensive, so they happen rarely and cover very little. Anyone with a dashcam is already carrying the sensor, which matters most on the roads official surveys never reach. A smoother route is also an accessibility question for anyone on two wheels, in a wheelchair, or carrying a patient.

Road Validation, a labeling game

Crowdsourced dataset · 53 contributors · 2025
↗

The road model needed a large set of labeled examples. Rather than pay strangers by the piece, I made it a game. You are shown a frame from a dashcam and rate the road against a five-point rubric, from flawless to severe damage, and you only score points when someone else independently gives the same answer. Fifty-three people joined in, and the result is a dataset of 3,216 images that each had independent agreement. The most dedicated contributor labeled 1,659 frames.

Why it matters

A model is only ever as good as the labels underneath it, and paying by the piece buys speed rather than care. Rewarding agreement instead of volume makes accuracy the thing worth chasing, and it turned a solitary research chore into something people actually wanted to do.

annotate.anishsheela.com

ApplyQuest

Full-stack platform · live demo · 2026
↗

A job hunt is hundreds of applications spread over months, with long silences and very little feedback. ApplyQuest keeps all of it in one place: where each application stands, who you have spoken to, what needs a follow up this week. It borrows from games, awarding points and streaks for the effort you control, because the outcome mostly is not. A browser add-on saves a posting in one click, a weekly email tells you where things stand, and an MCP server lets an AI assistant check your tracked applications against your actual Gmail inbox. The demo is loaded with a sample job hunt, so you can click straight in.

Why it matters

Job hunting is demoralizing precisely because effort and results come apart. Rewarding the applications you sent, rather than the replies you did not get, is what keeps people going through the part that actually decides whether they get hired.

Jarvis, a home server that rebuilds itself

Home infrastructure · NixOS · 2025 to 2026
↗

A Raspberry Pi that runs the household: media, automatic Mac backups, monitoring, remote access. The whole machine is described in one set of text files, so if the memory card dies I write a fresh one and get the identical server back. Storage lands on the fast drive first and drains to the big slow one in the background, and an unplugged disk degrades the server rather than stopping it. A cheap tablet on the wall shows the day from it: weather, tram departures, the shopping list, and it answers to "hey jarvis" without a word said in the house leaving the Pi.

github.com/anishanilkumar/jarvis
